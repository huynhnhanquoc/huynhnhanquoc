# InPublic

> **A philosophy for creating openly on the Internet.**

InPublic is not a startup.

It is not a social network.

It is not another blogging platform.

It is not another documentation website.

It is not another community for founders.

Instead, **InPublic is a philosophy.**

A philosophy that encourages people to openly create, learn, document, teach, and share what they know.

It believes the Internet becomes more valuable every time someone decides to make something public.

The purpose of InPublic is simple:

> **Help more people contribute to the Internet instead of only consuming it.**

---

# Why InPublic?

Every day, millions of people create something meaningful.

A developer solves a difficult bug.

A designer discovers a better workflow.

A student finally understands a complex concept.

A founder builds a side project.

A researcher finishes an experiment.

A writer drafts an article.

An artist creates something beautiful.

Most of these creations disappear.

They remain inside notebooks.

Private repositories.

Draft folders.

Desktop screenshots.

Local files.

Temporary notes.

Years later, almost nothing remains.

Not because they lacked value.

Because they were never shared.

The Internet grows because people decide to contribute.

Every public article expands collective knowledge.

Every open-source repository helps another developer.

Every tutorial shortens someone else's journey.

Every benchmark saves someone hours.

Every design inspires another designer.

Every honest story gives another person the courage to begin.

InPublic exists because valuable work deserves to be discovered instead of forgotten.

---

# The Philosophy

Creating publicly is not about becoming famous.

It is not about chasing followers.

It is not about marketing.

It is not about collecting likes.

It is not about building a personal brand.

Those things may happen.

They are simply not the purpose.

Creating publicly is about contributing.

It is about documenting.

It is about learning.

It is about helping.

It is about leaving useful traces behind.

The Internet remembers what people publish.

Not what they keep to themselves.

---

# What Does "In Public" Mean?

"In Public" is not a methodology.

A way of thinking.

A way of learning.

# Principles

Everything within InPublic is guided by a few simple principles.

## Contribute Before You Promote

Create something valuable before asking for attention.

Value should always come first.

---

## Progress Is Worth Sharing

Not everything needs to be finished.

Ideas become stronger through feedback.

People learn from progress just as much as success.

---

## Documentation Is Contribution

A README.

A tutorial.

A benchmark.

A design decision.

A mistake.

A lesson learned.

Documentation is not a by-product.

Documentation is part of the work.

---

## Consistency Beats Perfection

The Internet rewards people who continue creating.

You do not need one perfect masterpiece.

You need years of honest contributions.

---

## Teach What You Learn

You do not need to become an expert before helping someone.

Share what you learned today.

Someone else may need it tomorrow.

---

## Leave Useful Traces Behind

Create things that still have value years later.

Articles.

Repositories.

Videos.

Experiments.

Designs.

Guides.

Stories.

Knowledge compounds when it is public.

# Design Philosophy

The design should reflect the philosophy.

Simple.

Calm.

Timeless.

Readable.

Thoughtful.

Human.

The content should always be the hero.

Typography should lead the experience.

Whitespace should create focus.

Motion should support reading instead of distracting from it.

The website should never feel like a SaaS dashboard.

It should feel like a place people enjoy returning to.

---

# Writing Philosophy

Write like a creator speaking to another creator.

Avoid marketing language.

Avoid startup clichés.

Avoid exaggerated promises.

Teach through examples.

Explain through stories.

Encourage through honesty.

Be practical.

Be humble.

Be useful.
